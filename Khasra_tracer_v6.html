Khasra tracer

Turn Bhunaksha screenshots into khasra polygons you can open in GIS.

Split view: Bhunaksha + map

Zoom Bhunaksha to your khasra first. To calibrate: click a spot in Bhunaksha (Click mode off) so it shows its coordinates and copy them, then turn Click mode on, click that same spot and paste the coordinates in the box below. Do this for two spots far apart diagonally. After that, each click on Bhunaksha drops a corner (A, B, C, D…) on the map with its lat/long. If you pan or zoom Bhunaksha, press Recalibrate.

Open in window

If the left pane stays blank or says "refused to connect", the site does not allow being shown inside another page. Open it in a window beside this one instead.

Desktop Chrome or Edge. Click a point in Bhunaksha so it shows its coordinates, press this, and choose "This tab". Only the Bhunaksha pane is read, and only coordinates are kept.

Text read from the screen (to check the digits)


Georeference to lat/long: enter the easting and northing (UTM, zone as set in step 2) for each reference spot and press its button. The lat/long is filled in for you.












Add a Bhunaksha or other overlay (optional)









1. Load a screenshot


Screenshot a zoomed-in part of Bhunaksha HP. Plot lines should be clear and not covered by panels.















Crop (in pixels) removes the browser bar and side panels so only the map is traced.







2. Match it to the ground


In Bhunaksha, tap two plot corners far apart and note the Lat/Lng numbers shown each time. Then press Place ref 1, tap that same corner on the screenshot below, and enter its numbers. Repeat for ref 2. (Not needed if you use the live map in step 5.)





















3. Find the parcels























4. Number and save


Tap a parcel on the screenshot, type its khasra number, then press Set number. Compare the area shown with the area in Plot Info.







Collection is empty. Overlap your screenshots a little so edge parcels are caught whole.




Paste the text into a file ending in .geojson or .kml, then open it in QGIS, Google Earth or geojson.io.
