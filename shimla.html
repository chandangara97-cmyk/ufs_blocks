About Shimla

Shimla, the state capital, served as the summer capital of British India from 1864. The district blends Victorian architecture — the Viceregal Lodge, Christ Church, Gaiety Theatre — with dense cedar and rhododendron forests. Beyond the city, Shimla district offers Narkanda for skiing, Rampur for tribal culture, Chail for the world's highest cricket ground, and Rohru for off-the-beaten-path apple orchards.

The Ridge & Mall Road

2,213 mShimla town centre
  • Ceremonial and social heart of British Shimla, summer capital of India from 1864 to 1939
  • Christ Church (1857), one of the oldest churches in northern India, anchors the eastern end
  • Gaiety Theatre (1887) on Mall Road still hosts live performances
  • Traffic-free promenade with panoramic views of the Shivalik and Dhauladhar ranges

Jakhu Temple

2,455 m2 km trek from the Ridge
  • Highest point of the Shimla ridge; one of Himachal's most visited Hanuman temples
  • Forest trail home to a large, bold population of rhesus monkeys
  • Ropeway (gondola) links the Ridge to the hilltop
  • 33-metre Hanuman statue installed in 2010, visible across central Shimla

Viceregal Lodge

Observatory HillCompleted 1888
  • Former official summer residence of the British Viceroy; now Indian Institute of Advanced Study
  • Scottish baronial architecture in grey limestone, with terraced gardens
  • Hosted the Shimla Conference of 1945, which shaped the Partition of India
  • Guided tours through the state rooms, library and Viceregal office

2,622 m16 km from Shimla

  • Himalayan Nature Park: snow leopards, Himalayan brown bears, pheasants

  • Drive passes through apple orchards, best in late September

Narkanda

2,708 mOld Hindustan-Tibet Road
  • One of Shimla district's highest towns and a major apple-growing belt
  • Orchards bloom white in May; harvest crates line the roads Aug–Oct
  • Government ski slopes on Hatu ridge, open January–March
  • Hatu Peak temple (3,400 m) a 7 km drive away

Chail

2,250 mSolan / Shimla border
  • Created in 1891 by Maharaja Bhupinder Singh of Patiala
  • Chail Palace, now a heritage hotel run by Himachal Tourism
  • Chail Cricket Ground (1893) — Guinness-certified as the world's highest cricket ground
  • Forest sanctuary with pheasants, barking deer and leopard

Rohru

~26 km from Shimla

Hatkoti (Pabbar Valley)

95 km from ShimlaPabbar Valley
  • Known as the 'valley of temples' for its density of ancient shrines
  • Durga and Shiva temples in Shikhara style, from three historical periods
  • Legendary founding attributed to Shankaracharya; reroofed under Raja Padam Chandra of Jubbal
  • Rare bronze Mahishasurmardini Durga image; torna with a 9th-century inscription
  • Protected by the Archaeological Survey of India
  • Pabbar river here is a recognised trout fishery
