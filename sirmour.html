About Sirmour

Sirmour is Himachal's southernmost district, bordering Haryana and Uttarakhand. This place is the least touristed of the accessible districts — making it a genuine offbeat destination. Renuka Lake (the largest natural lake in HP) is sacred to Lord Parashurama. The Shivalik fossil park near Suketi contains million-year-old vertebrate fossils. Churdhar (3,647 m) is the highest peak in the outer Himalayas.

Top Places to Visit (10)

Renuka Lake

3,214 m
  • The largest natural lake in Himachal Pradesh at 3,214 m length and up to 672 m width, Renuka is shaped like the reclining form of the goddess Renuka — the mother of the warrior-sage Parashurama — and this resemblance is the source of its sacred identity.
  • A smaller lake adjacent to the main body is said to be Parashurama himself, watching over his mother.
  • The annual Renuka Fair (November–December, at the Kartik Purnima full moon) is one of Himachal's most important religious gatherings, drawing over 200,000 pilgrims over five days.
  • The lakeside has a modest zoo with a lion, tiger and leopard enclosure, a boating facility, and walking paths.
  • The surrounding forest is habitat for leopard and sloth bear.

Churdhar Peak

3,647 m14 km trek from Nohradhar or Haripurdhar for darshan
  • At 3,647 m, Churdhar is the highest peak of the outer Himalayan range — the Shivalik system — and the highest point reachable by trekking in the districts of Sirmour and Sirmaur without crossing into the inner ranges.
  • The Shirgul Maharaj temple on the summThis place is served by a priest who stays from May to October; pilgrims from the Sirmour and Shimla foothills complete the 14 km trek from Nohradhar or Haripurdhar for darshan.
  • The route passes through remarkable forest transitions: subtropical thorn scrub at the base gives way to mixed subtropical forest, then oak and rhododendron, then silver fir and finally the open alpine zone near the summit.
  • The 360° panorama from the top extends from the Shivalik plains to the Kinnaur Kailash range.

Nahan

932 m
  • Founded in 1621 by Raja Karam Prakash of the Sirmour kingdom on a commanding ridge at 932 m, Nahan is one of Himachal Pradesh's finest examples of a planned hill town — its street grid, central market square and palace complex were laid out in a single design that remains largely intact.
  • The Rani Tal Garden, created by a Sirmour queen in the 19th century as a private pleasure garden, is now a public park of great charm.
  • The Jagannath temple complex on the town ridge has been an active pilgrimage site for four centuries.
  • Nahan's October–March climate — mild days and cool nights, minimal rainfall — makes it the most comfortable lowland town in Himachal and an underrated base for exploring southern Sirmour.

Suketi Fossil Park

  • Asia's first fossil park, established in 1974 at Suketi on the banks of the Markanda river, preserves evidence of the extraordinary Shivalik fossil fauna that inhabited this region between 5 and 8 million years ago when the foothills were open woodland and savannah.
  • Life-size concrete replicas of Stegodon (an ancient elephant relative), Sivatherium (a giant giraffe), the Shivalik hippopotamus and a Sivapithecus primate are displayed at the sites where their original fossils were excavated.
  • The adjoining fossil museum contains actual specimens including a complete Stegodon skull.
  • The landscape along the Markanda here is representative of the lower Shivalik — gravel river bars, scrub forest and the red-and-buff Siwalik geological formations.

Paonta Sahib

  • On the west bank of the Yamuna river at the Himachal Pradesh–Uttarakhand border, Paonta Sahib ranks among the most significant Sikh historical sites — the gurdwara marks the place where Guru Gobind Singh, the 10th Sikh Guru, lived from 1685 to 1689 and composed much of his literary work including the Dasam Granth.
  • The Langar (free community kitchen) at the gurdwara feeds thousands daily.
  • The Yamuna at Paonta Sahib is broad and calm in comparison to its upper reaches, and the riverside setting is pleasant.
  • The town is a significant pharmaceutical manufacturing centre — the 'pharma valley' around Paonta Sahib is among India's larger drug manufacturing clusters, a fact that gives the district an unusual economic character.

Haripurdhar

2,100 m
  • At approximately 2,100 m on the ridge above the Sirmour–Shimla watershed, Haripurdhar is the most accessible base for the Churdhar trek — typically a 2-day journey to the summit and back.
  • The village has a small bazaar, a primary health centre and basic guesthouses that fill with pilgrims in the summer trekking season.
  • The apple orchards around Haripurdhar produce good quality fruit in September–October.
  • The trail to Churdhar through the silver fir forest above the village is well-marked and in good condition in dry weather.
  • The ridge viewpoints above the village, accessible on shorter walks, offer views toward both the Shimla hills and the Sirmour plains.

Rajgarh

1,600 m60 km)
  • The Pabbar river valley between Rohru (Shimla district) and the Sirmour border passes through Rajgarh — a small administrative centre at approximately 1,600 m that is the commercial hub for the apple orchards filling this section of the valley.
  • The Pabbar here is an excellent brown trout fishery with a Forest Department permit system.
  • The valley landscape in October, with the apple harvest underway and the surrounding deciduous trees turning colour, is exceptionally scenic and almost entirely unvisited by tourists.
  • A road connects Rajgarh across the watershed to Renuka Lake (approximately 60 km), making a circuit possible for those with their own vehicle.

Sarahan (Sirmour)

  • Not to be confused with the more famous Sarahan in Kinnaur, this Sarahan in Sirmour district is a small village on the Girri river where the ancient Trilokpur Mata temple draws devotees from across the Shivalik foothills.
  • The temple, set in a forested gorge, is an important local pilgrimage centre for Sirmour's Hindu communities.
  • The Girri valley here is typical of the lower Sirmour landscape — narrow, humid and forested with subtropical species including bamboo and wild banana at lower elevations.
  • This area is undeveloped for tourism and largely visited by pilgrims rather than travellers.

Dadahu

  • A small market village on the Giri Ganga river in central Sirmour, Dadahu is primarily visited as a river camping base — the Giri Ganga in this section runs clear and swift through a forested gorge that is good for nature walks and seasonal fishing.
  • The surrounding oak forest supports leopard, barking deer and a variety of woodland birds.
  • The October–March period, when the weather is mild and clear and the forest is dry, is ideal for camp-based exploration.
  • Basic accommodation is available in the village; most visitors camp on the river flats.

Shillai

90 km) through continuous forest — the journey itself is as rewarding as the destination
  • On the Uttarakhand border at the southeastern tip of Sirmour district, Shillai is a remote sub-divisional headquarters in a landscape of dense oak and rhododendron forest that is among the least-visited terrain in Himachal Pradesh.
  • The terrain here transitions from the Himalayan foothills to the outer Garhwal ranges, and the forest is remarkably intact — leopard, goral, barking deer and the sloth bear are all present.
  • The spring wildflower display (March–May) in the oak and rhododendron forest below the ridge is spectacular and entirely undiscovered by tourism.
  • Shillai can be reached on a road from Paonta Sahib (approximately 90 km) through continuous forest — the journey itself is as rewarding as the destination.
